# 03 · RL

## The policy

- **Code:** `pokersim/rl_train.py`, `pokersim/rl_features.py` (193 inputs), `pokersim/rl_policy.py`.
- **Algorithm:** PPO with GAE. The hero sits in one seat of six, and opponents change every 50–300 hands.
- **Reward:** net big blinds / 10.
- **Actions (7):** fold, check/call, min raise, ½ pot, pot, 2× pot, all-in.
- **Inputs:**
  - the hero's cards, the board, the betting, stacks and positions;
  - each opponent's style, from the live tracker;
  - each opponent's predicted range: 9 summary numbers, plus equity against hands drawn from the hand model.

## Opponents

**Training, `--mix v3`:**
- Past copies of the policy: 25% of seats (from update 25).
- Of the remaining seats:
  - 40% are **pro bots**: the next-move model with its own cards, as the 13 Pluribus-log professionals play, each
    with a random temperature (0.8–1.2) and a fold/call/raise tilt (±0.5).
  - 60% are scripted bots: equity bots with random tightness and aggression, calling station, maniac, random, TAG,
    LAG.

**Held-out tables** (never in training; these alone choose the model):

| Table | Seats |
|---|---|
| `heldout_pluribus` | 5 Pluribus-cloned bots (`plur`, temperatures 0.9–1.1) |
| `heldout_rules` | The new rule bots: push/fold nit, slow-playing trapper, min-raiser, strict pot-odds caller |
| `heldout_mix` | Pluribus clones and rule bots mixed |

The pro and Pluribus modes share one network, so `heldout_pluribus` is not fully independent of the pro training
bots. It is still the closest we have to "unseen strong humans". `heldout_rules` is fully independent.

## Evaluation (every 50 updates)

- **Paired comparison.** Every hero (RL, RL without style inputs, RL without hand inputs, the equity bot) plays the
  same seeded deals, so differences are taken hand by hand: much tighter than comparing two averages.
  - In-distribution tables: 3,000 hands per hero.
  - Held-out tables: 10,000 hands per hero.
- **Selection score:** mean over the held-out tables of the paired per-hand difference RL − equity bot, in bb/100.
  - The best one is exported to `<run>/export_heldout/`.
  - Every best is also kept in `<run>/best_heldout_history/`.
- **The final evaluation uses different deals** from the ones that chose the model, to avoid a winner's curse.

## Runs

| Run | Machine | Start | Arguments |
|---|---|---|---|
| **v3-ranges** | 5090 PC, 14 workers | Fresh, seed 1 | `--mix v3` |
| **v3-noranges** | 2080 PC, 14 workers | Fresh, seed 1 | `--mix v3 --no-range-inputs` |
| **v2-eddie** | Eddie, 8 cores, `--cpu` | v2 update 56 | `--mix v2` (insurance) |

- **Comparing the two v3 runs:** same seed and opponents, so they are compared **at equal update numbers**, not equal
  time. The no-ranges run is faster per update.
- **What this measures:** "predicting hands helps against held-out opponents" without the out-of-distribution
  input-zeroing problem. The in-run "range inputs" figure (RL − RL with ranges zeroed) is reported only as "how much
  the policy relies on them".
- **Shipping:** we ship the best held-out export of whichever run wins. If neither beats v2 update 50 on the
  held-out tables, we ship v2.
